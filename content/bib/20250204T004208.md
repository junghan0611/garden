## 심볼릭 프로그래밍 {#심볼릭-프로그래밍}

In computer programming, symbolic programming is a programming paradigm in which the program can manipulate its own formulas and program components as if they were plain data.[1]

컴퓨터 프로그래밍에서 기호 프로그래밍은 프로그램이 자체 수식과 프로그램 구성 요소를 마치 일반 데이터처럼 조작할 수 있는 프로그래밍 패러다임입니다. [1]

Through symbolic programming, complex processes can be developed that build other more intricate processes by combining smaller units of logic or functionality. Thus, such programs can effectively modify themselves and appear to "learn", which makes them better suited for applications such as artificial intelligence, expert systems, natural language processing, and computer games.

기호 프로그래밍을 통해 더 작은 단위의 논리나 기능을 결합하여 더 복잡한 프로세스를 구축하는 복잡한 프로세스를 개발할 수 있습니다. 따라서 이러한 프로그램은 효과적으로 스스로를 수정하고 "학습"하는 것처럼 보일 수 있으므로 인공 지능, 전문가 시스템, 자연어 처리 및 컴퓨터 게임과 같은 애플리케이션에 더 적합합니다.

Languages that support symbolic programming include homoiconic languages such as Wolfram Language,[2] Lisp, Prolog,[3] and Julia.

기호 프로그래밍을 지원하는 언어에는 Wolfram 언어, [2] Lisp, Prolog, [3] 및 Julia와 같은 동형 언어가 있습니다.

### 요약: 기호적 인공지능 {#요약-기호적-인공지능}

(“기호주의 인공지능 Symbolic Artificial Intelligence” 2024) "기호주의 인공지능 Symbolic artificial intelligence"

```text
In artificial intelligence, symbolic artificial intelligence is the term for the collection of all methods in artificial intelligence research that are based on high-level symbolic (human-readable) representations of problems, logic and search. Symbolic AI used tools such as logic programming, production rules, semantic nets and frames, and it developed applications such as knowledge-based systems (in particular, expert systems), symbolic mathematics, automated theorem provers, ontologies, the semantic web, and automated planning and scheduling systems. The Symbolic AI paradigm led to seminal ideas in search, symbolic programming languages, agents, multi-agent systems, the semantic web, and the strengths and limitations of formal knowledge and reasoning systems. Symbolic AI was the dominant paradigm of AI research from the mid-1950s until the mid-1990s. Researchers in the 1960s and the 1970s were convinced that symbolic approaches would eventually succeed in creating a machine with artificial general intelligence and considered this the ultimate goal of their field. An early boom, with early successes such as the Logic Theorist and Samuel's Checkers Playing Program, led to unrealistic expectations and promises and was followed by the First AI Winter as funding dried up. A second boom (1969–1986) occurred with the rise of expert systems, their promise of capturing corporate expertise, and an enthusiastic corporate embrace. That boom, and some early successes, e.g., with XCON at DEC, was followed again by later disappointment. Problems with difficulties in knowledge acquisition, maintaining large knowledge bases, and brittleness in handling out-of-domain problems arose. Another, second, AI Winter (1988–2011) followed. Subsequently, AI researchers focused on addressing underlying problems in handling uncertainty and in knowledge acquisition. Uncertainty was addressed with formal methods such as hidden Markov models, Bayesian reasoning, and statistical relational learning. Symbolic machine learning addressed the knowledge acquisition problem with contributions including Version Space, Valiant's PAC learning, Quinlan's ID3 decision-tree learning, case-based learning, and inductive logic programming to learn relations. Neural networks, a subsymbolic approach, had been pursued from early days and reemerged strongly in 2012. Early examples are Rosenblatt's perceptron learning work, the backpropagation work of Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams, and work in convolutional neural networks by LeCun et al. in 1989. However, neural networks were not viewed as successful until about 2012: "Until Big Data became commonplace, the general consensus in the Al community was that the so-called neural-network approach was hopeless. Systems just didn't work that well, compared to other methods. ... A revolution came in 2012, when a number of people, including a team of researchers working with Hinton, worked out a way to use the power of GPUs to enormously increase the power of neural networks." Over the next several years, deep learning had spectacular success in handling vision, speech recognition, speech synthesis, image generation, and machine translation. However, since 2020, as inherent difficulties with bias, explanation, comprehensibility, and robustness became more apparent with deep learning approaches; an increasing number of AI researchers have called for combining the best of both the symbolic and neural network approaches and addressing areas that both approaches have difficulty with, such as common-sense reasoning.
```

두 번째 붐(1969~1986년)은 전문가 시스템의 등장, 기업의 전문성을 확보할 수 있다는 약속, 기업의 열광적인 수용과 함께 일어났습니다. 이러한 붐과 DEC의 XCON과 같은 일부 초기 성공은 이후 다시 실망으로 이어졌습니다. 지식 습득의 어려움, 대규모 지식 기반 유지, 도메인 외 문제 처리의 취약성 등의 문제가 발생했습니다. 또 다른 두 번째 AI 겨울(1988~2011년)이 이어졌습니다. 이후 AI 연구자들은 불확실성 처리와 지식 습득의 근본적인 문제를 해결하는 데 집중했습니다. 불확실성은 숨겨진 마르코프 모델, 베이지안 추론, 통계적 관계 학습과 같은 공식적인 방법으로 해결되었습니다. 기호적 기계 학습은 버전 스페이스, Valiant의 PAC 학습, Quinlan의 ID3 의사 결정 트리 학습, 사례 기반 학습, 관계 학습을 위한 귀납적 논리 프로그래밍 등의 기여를 통해 지식 습득 문제를 해결했습니다.

하위 기호적 접근 방식인 신경망은 초기부터 추구되어 왔으며 2012년에 다시 크게 부상했습니다. 초기 사례로는 1989년 Rosenblatt의 퍼셉트론 학습 연구, Rumelhart, Hinton, Williams의 역전파 연구, LeCun 등의 컨볼루션 신경망 연구 등이 있습니다. 그러나 신경망은 2012년경까지만 해도 성공적이라고 여겨지지 않았습니다: "빅 데이터가 보편화되기 전까지는 소위 신경망 접근 방식은 절망적이라는 것이 Al 커뮤니티의 일반적인 합의였습니다. 다른 방법에 비해 시스템이 잘 작동하지 않았기 때문입니다. ... 2012년에 힌튼과 함께 일하는 연구팀을 비롯한 많은 사람들이 GPU의 성능을 이용해 신경망의 성능을 엄청나게 향상시키는 방법을 찾아내면서 혁명이 일어났습니다." 그 후 몇 년 동안 딥러닝은 비전, 음성 인식, 음성 합성, 이미지 생성, 기계 번역을 처리하는 데 눈부신 성공을 거두었습니다. 그러나 2020년 이후 편향성, 설명력, 이해도, 견고성 등 딥러닝 접근 방식에 내재된 어려움이 드러나면서 점점 더 많은 AI 연구자들이 기호적 접근 방식과 신경망 접근 방식의 장점을 결합하고 상식적인 추론과 같이 두 접근 방식이 모두 어려움을 겪는 영역을 해결해야 한다고 주장하고 있습니다.

In [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence), **symbolic artificial intelligence** is the term for the collection of all methods in artificial intelligence research that are based on high-level [symbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_symbol_systems_hypothesis) (human-readable) representations of problems, [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) and [search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_algorithm).^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-1]] Symbolic AI used tools such as [logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_programming), [production rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_(computer_science)), [semantic nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_nets) and [frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_(artificial_intelligence)), and it developed applications such as [knowledge-based systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge-based_systems) (in particular, [expert systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expert_systems)), [symbolic mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_mathematics), [automated theorem provers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_provers), [ontologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontologies), the [semantic web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_web), and [automated planning and scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_planning_and_scheduling) systems. The Symbolic AI paradigm led to seminal ideas in [search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence#Search_and_optimization), symbolic programming languages, [agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_agent), [multi-agent systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_systems), the [semantic web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_web), and the strengths and limitations of formal knowledge and [reasoning systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_reasoning).

Symbolic AI was the dominant [paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm) of AI research from the mid-1950s until the mid-1990s.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKolata1982-2]] Researchers in the 1960s and the 1970s were convinced that symbolic approaches would eventually succeed in creating a machine with [artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence) and considered this the ultimate goal of their field.^[/[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)/] An early boom, with early successes such as the [Logic Theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_Theorist) and [Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Samuel_(computer_scientist))'s [Checkers Playing Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Samuel_(computer_scientist)), led to unrealistic expectations and promises and was followed by the First [AI Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_winter) as funding dried up.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022107–109-3]]^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTERussellNorvig202119-4]] A second boom (1969–1986) occurred with the rise of expert systems, their promise of capturing corporate expertise, and an enthusiastic corporate embrace.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTERussellNorvig202122–23-5]]^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022109–110-6]] That boom, and some early successes, e.g., with [XCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XCON) at [DEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Equipment_Corporation), was followed again by later disappointment.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022109–110-6]] Problems with difficulties in knowledge acquisition, maintaining large knowledge bases, and brittleness in handling out-of-domain problems arose. Another, second, AI Winter (1988–2011) followed.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022110-7]] Subsequently, AI researchers focused on addressing underlying problems in handling uncertainty and in knowledge acquisition.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022110–111-8]] Uncertainty was addressed with formal methods such as [hidden Markov models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Markov_model), [Bayesian reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_reasoning), and [statistical relational learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_relational_learning).^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTERussellNorvig202125-9]]^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2022111-10]] Symbolic machine learning addressed the knowledge acquisition problem with contributions including [Version Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_space_learning), [Valiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Valiant)'s [PAC learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probably_approximately_correct_learning), [Quinlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Quinlan)'s [ID3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ID3_algorithm) [decision-tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-tree) learning, [case-based learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-based_reasoning), and [inductive logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_logic_programming) to learn relations.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2020110–111-11]]

[Neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network), a subsymbolic approach, had been pursued from early days and reemerged strongly in 2012. Early examples are [Rosenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rosenblatt)'s [perceptron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceptron) learning work, the [backpropagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpropagation) work of Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams,^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-12]] and work in [convolutional neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolutional_neural_network) by LeCun et al. in 1989.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-13]] However, neural networks were not viewed as successful until about 2012: "Until Big Data became commonplace, the general consensus in the Al community was that the so-called neural-network approach was hopeless. Systems just didn't work that well, compared to other methods. ... A revolution came in 2012, when a number of people, including a team of researchers working with Hinton, worked out a way to use the power of [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPUs) to enormously increase the power of neural networks."^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarcusDavis2019-14]] Over the next several years, [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) had spectacular success in handling vision, [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition), speech synthesis, image generation, and machine translation. However, since 2020, as inherent difficulties with bias, explanation, comprehensibility, and robustness became more apparent with deep learning approaches; an increasing number of AI researchers have called for [combining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-symbolic_AI) the best of both the symbolic and neural network approaches^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-Rossi-15]]^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-Selman-16]] and addressing areas that both approaches have difficulty with, such as [common-sense reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonsense_reasoning).^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarcusDavis2019-14]]

A short history of symbolic AI to the present day follows below. Time periods and titles are drawn from Henry Kautz's 2020 AAAI Robert S. Engelmore Memorial Lecture^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTEKautz2020-17]] and the longer Wikipedia article on the [History of AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_AI), with dates and titles differing slightly for increased clarity.


### Techniques and contributions {#techniques-and-contributions}


#### AI programming languages[edit] {#ai-programming-languages-edit}

The key AI programming language in the US during the last symbolic AI boom period was [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP_(programming_language)). [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP_(programming_language)) is the second oldest programming language after [FORTRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORTRAN) and was created in 1958 by [John McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCarthy_(computer_scientist)). LISP provided the first [read-eval-print loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read-eval-print_loop) to support rapid program development. Compiled functions could be freely mixed with interpreted functions. Program tracing, stepping, and breakpoints were also provided, along with the ability to change values or functions and continue from breakpoints or errors. It had the first [self-hosting compiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-hosting_(compilers)), meaning that the compiler itself was originally written in LISP and then ran interpretively to compile the compiler code.

지난 상징적 인 AI 붐 기간 동안 미국의 주요 AI 프로그래밍 언어는 [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP_(%ED%94%84%EB%A1%9C%EA%B7%B8%EB%9E%98%EB%B0%8D%20%EC%96%B8%EC%96%B4))였습니다. [<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP_(%ED%94%84%EB%A1%9C%EA%B7%B8%EB%9E%98%EB%B0%8D_%EC%96%B8%EC%96%B4)>][LISP]]는 [포트란](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORTRAN) 다음으로 오래된 프로그래밍 언어로, [존 맥카시]가 1958년에 만들었습니다. LISP는 빠른 프로그램 개발을 지원하기 위해 최초의 [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read-eval-print_loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCarthy_(%EC%BB%B4%ED%93%A8%ED%84%B0_%EA%B3%BC%ED%95%99%EC%9E%90))[읽기-평가-출력 루프]]를 제공했습니다. 컴파일된 함수는 해석된 함수와 자유롭게 혼합될 수 있었습니다. 프로그램 추적, 스테핑, 중단점도 제공되었으며, 값이나 함수를 변경하고 중단점이나 오류에서 계속할 수 있는 기능도 제공되었습니다. 최초의 <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-hosting_(%EC%BB%B4%ED%8C%8C%EC%9D%BC%EB%9F%AC)> [BROKEN LINK: 자체 호스팅 컴파일러]가 있었는데, 이는 컴파일러 자체가 원래 LISP로 작성된 후 해석적으로 실행되어 컴파일러 코드를 컴파일하는 것을 의미합니다.

Other key innovations pioneered by LISP that have spread to other programming languages include:

-   [Garbage collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbage_collection_(computer_science))
-   [Dynamic typing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_typing)
-   [Higher-order functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_function)
-   [Recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion)
-   [Conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_(computer_programming))

Programs were themselves data structures that other programs could operate on, allowing the easy definition of higher-level languages.

In contrast to the US, in Europe the key AI programming language during that same period was [Prolog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolog). Prolog provided a built-in store of facts and clauses that could be queried by a [read-eval-print loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read-eval-print_loop). The store could act as a knowledge base and the clauses could act as rules or a restricted form of logic. As a subset of first-order logic Prolog was based on [Horn clauses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_clauses) with a [closed-world assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-world_assumption)—any facts not known were considered false—and a [unique name assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_name_assumption) for primitive terms—e.g., the identifier barack_obama was considered to refer to exactly one object. [Backtracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backtracking) and [unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_(computer_science)) are built-in to Prolog.

[Alain Colmerauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Colmerauer) and Philippe Roussel are credited as the inventors of Prolog. Prolog is a form of logic programming, which was invented by [Robert Kowalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kowalski). Its history was also influenced by [Carl Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Hewitt)'s [PLANNER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLANNER), an assertional database with pattern-directed invocation of methods. For more detail see the [section on the origins of Prolog in the PLANNER article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planner_(programming_language)#The_genesis_of_Prolog).

Prolog is also a kind of [declarative programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declarative_programming). The logic clauses that describe programs are directly interpreted to run the programs specified. No explicit series of actions is required, as is the case with [imperative programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperative_programming) languages.

Japan championed Prolog for its [Fifth Generation Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Generation_Project), intending to build special hardware for high performance. Similarly, [LISP machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP_machines) were built to run LISP, but as the second AI boom turned to bust these companies could not compete with new workstations that could now run LISP or Prolog natively at comparable speeds. See the [BROKEN LINK: A_short_history] for more detail.

[Smalltalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smalltalk) was another influential AI programming language. For example, it introduced [metaclasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaclasses) and, along with [Flavors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavors_(programming_language)) and [CommonLoops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CommonLoops), influenced the [Common Lisp Object System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Lisp_Object_System), or ([CLOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLOS)), that is now part of [Common Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Lisp), the current standard Lisp dialect. [CLOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLOS) is a Lisp-based object-oriented system that allows [multiple inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_inheritance), in addition to incremental extensions to both classes and metaclasses, thus providing a run-time [meta-object protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-object_protocol).^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-meta-object_protocol-87]]

For other AI programming languages see this [list of programming languages for artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programming_languages_for_artificial_intelligence). Currently, [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_(programming_language)), a [multi-paradigm programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-paradigm_programming_language), is the most popular programming language, partly due to its extensive package library that supports [data science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_science), natural language processing, and deep learning. [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_(programming_language)) includes a read-eval-print loop, functional elements such as [higher-order functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_function), and [object-oriented programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming) that includes metaclasses.


#### Search[edit] {#search-edit}

Search arises in many kinds of problem solving, including [planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_planning), [constraint satisfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_satisfaction), and playing games such as [checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkers), [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess), and [go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_(game)). The best known AI-search tree search algorithms are [breadth-first search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadth-first_search), [depth-first search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth-first_search), [A\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A*_search_algorithm), and [Monte Carlo Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_tree_search). Key search algorithms for [Boolean satisfiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_satisfiability) are [WalkSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WalkSAT), [conflict-driven clause learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict-driven_clause_learning), and the [DPLL algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPLL_algorithm). For adversarial search when playing games, [alpha-beta pruning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-beta_pruning), [branch and bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_and_bound), and [minimax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimax) were early contributions.


#### Knowledge representation and reasoning[edit] {#knowledge-representation-and-reasoning-edit}

Multiple different approaches to represent knowledge and then reason with those representations have been investigated. Below is a quick overview of approaches to knowledge representation and automated reasoning.

<!--list-separator-->

-  Knowledge representation[edit]

    [Semantic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_networks), [conceptual graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_graphs), [frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_(artificial_intelligence)), and [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) are all approaches to modeling knowledge such as domain knowledge, problem-solving knowledge, and the semantic meaning of language. [Ontologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontologies) model key concepts and their relationships in a domain. Example ontologies are [YAGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YAGO_(database)), [WordNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WordNet), and [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE). [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE) is an example of an [upper ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology) that can be used for any domain while WordNet is a lexical resource that can also be viewed as an [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#WordNet). YAGO incorporates WordNet as part of its ontology, to align facts extracted from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia) with WordNet [synsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synsets). The [Disease Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_Ontology) is an example of a medical ontology currently being used.

    [의미 네트워크](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_networks), [개념 그래프](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_graphs), [프레임](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_(%EC%9D%B8%EA%B3%B5%20%EC%A7%80%EB%8A%A5)), [논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic)는 모두 도메인 지식, 문제 해결 지식, 언어의 의미론적 의미와 같은 지식을 모델링하는 접근 방식입니다. [온톨로지](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontologies)는 도메인에서 주요 개념과 그 관계를 모델링합니다. 온톨로지의 예로는 [YAGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YAGO_(%EB%8D%B0%EC%9D%B4%ED%84%B0%EB%B2%A0%EC%9D%B4%EC%8A%A4)), [워드넷](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WordNet), [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE) 등이 있습니다. [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE)는 모든 도메인에 사용할 수 있는 [상위 온톨로지](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology)의 예이며, 워드넷은 [온톨로지](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#WordNet)로도 볼 수 있는 어휘 리소스입니다. YAGO는 [위키백과](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia)에서 추출한 사실을 워드넷 [싱크셋](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synsets)과 일치시키기 위해 워드넷을 온톨로지의 일부로 통합합니다. [질병 온톨로지](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_Ontology)는 현재 사용되고 있는 의료 온톨로지의 한 예입니다.

    [Description logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Description_logic) is a logic for automated classification of ontologies and for detecting inconsistent classification data. [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language) is a language used to represent ontologies with [description logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Description_logic). [Protégé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prot%C3%A9g%C3%A9_(software)) is an ontology editor that can read in [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language) ontologies and then check consistency with [deductive classifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_classifier) such as such as HermiT.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-HermiT-88]]

    [설명 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Description_logic)는 온톨로지를 자동으로 분류하고 일관되지 않은 분류 데이터를 탐지하기 위한 논리입니다. OWL은 [설명 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Description_logic)로 온톨로지를 표현하는 데 사용되는 언어입니다. 프로테제는 [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language) 온톨로지를 읽은 다음 [연역적 분류기](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_classifier)와 같은 HermiT과의 일관성을 확인할 수 있는 온톨로지 편집기입니다

    First-order logic is more general than description logic. The automated theorem provers discussed below can prove theorems in first-order logic. [Horn clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_clause) logic is more restricted than first-order logic and is used in logic programming languages such as Prolog. Extensions to first-order logic include [temporal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_logic), to handle time; [epistemic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic), to reason about agent knowledge; [modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic), to handle possibility and necessity; and [probabilistic logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_logic) to handle logic and probability together.

    일차 논리는 설명 논리보다 더 일반적입니다. 아래에 설명된 자동화된 정리 증명기는 일차 논리의 정리를 증명할 수 있습니다. Horn clause 논리는 일차 논리보다 더 제한적이며 프롤로그와 같은 논리 프로그래밍 언어에서 사용됩니다. 일차 논리의 확장으로는 시간을 다루는 [시간 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_logic), 에이전트 지식을 추론하는 [인식 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic), 가능성과 필요성을 다루는 [모달 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic), 논리와 확률을 함께 처리하는 [확률 논리](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_logic) 등이 있습니다.

<!--list-separator-->

-  Automatic theorem proving

    Examples of automated theorem provers for first-order logic are:

    -   [Prover9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prover9)
    -   [ACL2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACL2)
    -   [Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_(theorem_prover))

    [Prover9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prover9) can be used in conjunction with the [Mace4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mace4) [model checker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_checking). [ACL2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACL2) is a theorem prover that can handle proofs by induction and is a descendant of the Boyer-Moore Theorem Prover, also known as [Nqthm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nqthm).

    귀납법에 의한 증명을 처리할 수 있는 정리 증명기로, [Nqthm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nqthm)으로도 알려진 보이어-무어 정리 증명기의 후손입니다.

<!--list-separator-->

-  Reasoning in knowledge-based systems[edit]

    Knowledge-based systems have an explicit [knowledge base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_base), typically of rules, to enhance reusability across domains by separating procedural code and domain knowledge. A separate [inference engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_engine) processes rules and adds, deletes, or modifies a knowledge store.

    지식 기반 시스템에는 일반적으로 규칙으로 구성된 명시적인 [지식 기반](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_base)이 있어 절차 코드와 도메인 지식을 분리하여 도메인 간 재사용성을 향상시킵니다. 별도의 [추론 엔진](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_engine)이 규칙을 처리하고 지식 저장소를 추가, 삭제 또는 수정합니다.

    [Forward chaining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_chaining) inference engines are the most common, and are seen in [CLIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLIPS) and [OPS5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPS5). [Backward chaining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_chaining) occurs in Prolog, where a more limited logical representation is used, [Horn Clauses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_clause). Pattern-matching, specifically [unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_(computer_science)), is used in Prolog.

    [포워드 체인](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_chaining) 추론 엔진이 가장 일반적이며 [CLIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLIPS) 및 [OPS5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPS5)에서 볼 수 있습니다. [역연쇄](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_chaining)는 [혼절](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_clause)이라는 보다 제한된 논리적 표현이 사용되는 프롤로그에서 발생합니다. 패턴 매칭, 특히 [통일](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_(%EC%BB%B4%ED%93%A8%ED%84%B0_%EA%B3%BC%ED%95%99))은 Prolog에서 사용됩니다.

    A more flexible kind of problem-solving occurs when reasoning about what to do next occurs, rather than simply choosing one of the available actions. This kind of meta-level reasoning is used in Soar and in the BB1 blackboard architecture.

    단순히 사용 가능한 작업 중 하나를 선택하는 것이 아니라 다음에 수행할 작업에 대해 추론할 때 더 유연한 문제 해결이 가능합니다. 이러한 종류의 메타 수준 추론은 Soar와 BB1 블랙보드 아키텍처에서 사용됩니다.

    Cognitive architectures such as ACT-R may have additional capabilities, such as the ability to compile frequently used knowledge into higher-level [chunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunking_(psychology)).

    ACT-R과 같은 인지 아키텍처는 자주 사용하는 지식을 더 높은 수준의 [청크](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunking_(%EC%8B%AC%EB%A6%AC%ED%95%99))로 컴파일하는 기능과 같은 추가 기능을 가질 수 있습니다.

<!--list-separator-->

-  Commonsense reasoning[edit]

    [Marvin Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky) first proposed frames as a way of interpreting common visual situations, such as an office, and Roger Schank extended this idea to [scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Script_theory) for common routines, such as dining out. [Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc) has attempted to capture useful common-sense knowledge and has "micro-theories" to handle particular kinds of domain-specific reasoning.

    Qualitative simulation, such as [Benjamin Kuipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Kuipers)'s QSIM,^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-QSIM-89]] approximates human reasoning about naive physics, such as what happens when we heat a liquid in a pot on the stove. We expect it to heat and possibly boil over, even though we may not know its temperature, its boiling point, or other details, such as atmospheric pressure.

    Similarly, [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_F._Allen_(computer_scientist))'s [temporal interval algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen%27s_interval_algebra) is a simplification of reasoning about time and [Region Connection Calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Connection_Calculus) is a simplification of reasoning about spatial relationships. Both can be solved with [constraint solvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_programming).

<!--list-separator-->

-  Constraints and constraint-based reasoning[edit]

    Constraint solvers perform a more limited kind of inference than first-order logic. They can simplify sets of spatiotemporal constraints, such as those for [RCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_connection_calculus) or [Temporal Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen%27s_interval_algebra), along with solving other kinds of puzzle problems, such as [Wordle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle), [Sudoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudoku), [cryptarithmetic problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbal_arithmetic), and so on. [Constraint logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_logic_programming) can be used to solve scheduling problems, for example with [constraint handling rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_handling_rules) (CHR).


#### Automated planning[edit] {#automated-planning-edit}

The [General Problem Solver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Problem_Solver) (GPS) cast planning as problem-solving used [means-ends analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means-ends_analysis) to create plans. [STRIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Research_Institute_Problem_Solver) took a different approach, viewing planning as theorem proving. [Graphplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphplan) takes a least-commitment approach to planning, rather than sequentially choosing actions from an initial state, working forwards, or a goal state if working backwards. [Satplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satplan) is an approach to planning where a planning problem is reduced to a [Boolean satisfiability problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_satisfiability_problem).


#### Natural language processing {#natural-language-processing}

Natural language processing focuses on treating language as data to perform tasks such as identifying topics without necessarily understanding the intended meaning. Natural language understanding, in contrast, constructs a meaning representation and uses that for further processing, such as answering questions.

자연어 처리는 언어를 데이터로 취급하여 의도된 의미를 반드시 이해하지 않고도 주제 식별과 같은 작업을 수행하는 데 중점을 둡니다. 반면 자연어 이해는 의미 표현을 구성하고 이를 질문에 대한 답변과 같은 추가 처리에 사용합니다.

[Parsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsing), [tokenizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokenizing), [spelling correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spell_checker), [part-of-speech tagging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part-of-speech_tagging), [noun and verb phrase chunking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallow_parsing) are all aspects of natural language processing long handled by symbolic AI, but since improved by deep learning approaches. In symbolic AI, [discourse representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_representation_theory) and first-order logic have been used to represent sentence meanings. [Latent semantic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_semantic_analysis) (LSA) and [explicit semantic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explicit_semantic_analysis) also provided vector representations of documents. In the latter case, vector components are interpretable as concepts named by Wikipedia articles.

[구문 분석](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsing), [토큰화](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokenizing), [철자 교정](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spell_checker), [품사 태깅](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part-of-speech_tagging), [명사 및 동사 구문 청킹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallow_parsing)은 모두 오랫동안 상징적 AI가 처리해 왔지만 딥러닝 접근법으로 개선된 자연어 처리의 한 측면입니다. 기호 AI에서는 [담화 표현 이론](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_representation_theory)과 일차 논리가 문장의 의미를 표현하는 데 사용되었습니다. [잠재 의미 분석](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_semantic_analysis)(LSA)과 [명시적 의미 분석](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explicit_semantic_analysis)도 문서에 대한 벡터 표현을 제공했습니다. 후자의 경우, 벡터 구성 요소는 위키백과 문서에서 명명된 개념으로 해석할 수 있습니다.

New deep learning approaches based on [Transformer models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_(machine_learning_model)) have now eclipsed these earlier symbolic AI approaches and attained state-of-the-art performance in natural language _processing_. However, Transformer models are opaque and do not yet produce human-interpretable semantic representations for sentences and documents. Instead, they produce task-specific vectors where the meaning of the vector components is opaque.

[트랜스포머 모델](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_(machine_learning_model)) 에 기반한 새로운 딥러닝 접근 방식은 이제 이러한 초기의 상징적 AI 접근 방식을 뛰어넘어 자연어 /처리/에서 최첨단 성능을 달성했습니다. 그러나 트랜스포머 모델은 불투명하며 아직 문장과 문서에 대해 사람이 해석할 수 있는 의미적 표현을 생성하지 못합니다. 대신, 벡터 구성 요소의 의미가 불투명한 작업별 벡터를 생성합니다.


#### Agents and multi-agent systems[edit] {#agents-and-multi-agent-systems-edit}

[Agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_agent) are autonomous systems embedded in an environment they perceive and act upon in some sense. Russell and Norvig's standard textbook on artificial intelligence is organized to reflect agent architectures of increasing sophistication.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-FOOTNOTERussellNorvig2021-90]] The sophistication of agents varies from simple reactive agents, to those with a model of the world and [automated planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_planning) capabilities, possibly a [BDI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief%E2%80%93desire%E2%80%93intention_software_model), i.e., one with beliefs, desires, and intentions – or alternatively a [reinforcement learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning) model learned over time to choose actions – up to a combination of alternative architectures, such as a neuro-symbolic architecture^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-:0-86]] that includes deep learning for perception.^[BROKEN LINK: cite_note-91]]

In contrast, a [multi-agent system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_system) consists of multiple agents that communicate amongst themselves with some inter-agent communication language such as [Knowledge Query and Manipulation Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Query_and_Manipulation_Language) (KQML). The agents need not all have the same internal architecture. Advantages of multi-agent systems include the ability to divide work among the agents and to increase fault tolerance when agents are lost. Research problems include [how agents reach consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_dynamics), [distributed problem solving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_distributed_problem_solving), [multi-agent learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_learning), [multi-agent planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_planning), and [distributed constraint optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_constraint_optimization).


### Controversies[edit] {#controversies-edit}

Controversies arose from early on in symbolic AI, both within the field—e.g., between logicists (the pro-logic ["neats"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neats_and_scruffies)) and non-logicists (the anti-logic ["scruffies"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neats_and_scruffies))—and between those who embraced AI but rejected symbolic approaches—primarily [connectionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connectionism)—and those outside the field. Critiques from outside of the field were primarily from philosophers, on intellectual grounds, but also from funding agencies, especially during the two AI winters.
